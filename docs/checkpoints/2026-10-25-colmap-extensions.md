# Checkpoint 2026-10-25: COLMAP extensions, first full pass

Everything below was measured overnight on 2026-10-24/25 and is regenerated by
the commands at the end. Status words: **measured** (read from a report file),
**finding** (an interpretation of measured numbers), **not done**.

## What was built

| Extension | Hook in COLMAP | Code |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EXIF-anchored calibration | `IncrementalPipelineOptions.constant_cameras` and the focal-ratio window | `pipeline/ghostmap/sfm/calibration_variant.py` (mode `exif-anchored`) |
| Snap to lidar | similarity applied with `Reconstruction.transform` | `pipeline/ghostmap/anchor/{lidar,footprints,align}.py` |
| Cross-era resection with a certificate | `estimate_and_refine_absolute_pose` with the focal held fixed, over a focal grid | `pipeline/ghostmap/archival/{resect,match,run,people,modern_control}.py` |
| Live demo | — | `dist/ghostmap.html`, data from `pipeline/ghostmap/web/ghostmap_scene.py` |

New data: USGS 3DEP NY_FingerLakes_1_2020 lidar around the Quad (public domain,
2,525,265 points after cropping, ground and unclassified classes only), 58 OSM
building footprints (ODbL), and 104 more Commons photographs admitted through
location search (download still in progress, throttled by Commons).

## Measured results

### EXIF-anchored calibration (145-photo snapshot, 77 training photographs)

| Mapping | cameras per model | registered | focal ratio outside 0.5–3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| stock learned COLMAP | 21, 5, 3, 3, 2, 2 | 36 | 5 |
| radial distortion fixed at zero | 22, 20, 5, 3, 3, 2, 2 | 37 | 8 |
| EXIF-anchored | 22, 5, 3, 3, 2 | 35 | 2 |

**Finding:** holding EXIF focal lengths removes most focal blow-ups but does not
merge the fragments. Missing views, not calibration, split the scene.

### Snap to lidar (the 22-photographer Uris Library and McGraw Tower fragment)

- Gravity from facade normals; it differs by 14.6° from the camera up vectors,
  which photographers pitching up at the tower bias. The tower's long axis
  agrees with the facade estimate to within 1.6°.
- Scale from photographers at eye height against the measured tower top:
  **37.0 m per model unit**. Scale from the library's footprint walls alone:
  **35.0 m per unit**. The two independent estimates differ by **5.4 %**.
- 1,427 library points lie within 3 m of the library walls, median **0.50 m**.
- McGraw Tower measures **50.8 m** in the lidar (the real tower is about 53 m).
- Failed check: photographer heights above the lidar ground range from −19 m
  to +37 m (10th–90th percentile); a planar tilt explains only a quarter of the
  spread. See the next section.

Abandoned along the way (each recorded in the plan's decisions log):
correspondence-free wall matching in metres (shrinks the scene onto one
wall), the same with a coverage term (collapses onto the tower), model-unit
tolerance over every wall (prefers huge scales on a dense campus), and a free
tilt fitted to the photographers (17.8°, physically implausible).

### Positive control: the modern photographers themselves

Each photographer was re-resected from its own anchors with the focal length
unknown, exactly as a postcard would be. Median width of the 95 % interval of
the camera's position along its viewing ray:

| Anchors used | surviving anchors only | + measured ground |
| --- | --- | --- |
| all of the camera's anchors | 15.3 m (10 of 22 cameras over 20 m) | 4.2 m (6 over 20 m) |
| only the tower and nearby library facade (postcard-like) | 30.7 m (11 of 21 over 20 m) | 7.1 m (6 over 20 m) |

**Finding:** stock COLMAP leaves about half of these modern photographers
undetermined by more than 20 m along their viewing rays; that is why its bundle
adjustment can put them 30–50 m in the air or underground at under 2 px
reprojection error. The measured ground narrows the median interval by about
75 %. Caveat: even with the ground cue, most height intervals do not reach
standing height, so a residual systematic error remains in the frame or in the
cameras (not resolved).

16 of 19 photographs register through surviving buildings (at least 12 anchor
inliers). Width of the 95 % interval along the viewing ray:

| Date | Photograph | anchors | surviving anchors only | + measured ground | + people |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1934 | Boardman Hall and the library | 54 | 8 m | 5 m | — |
| 1934 | same card, second upload | 37 | 466 m | 43 m | — |
| 1909 | Boardman Hall and Library | 19 | 16 m | 3 m | — |
| c. 1903 | Boardman Hall and Library | 46 | 460 m | 47 m | 26 m |
| c. 1900 | Boardman Hall, Department of Law | 19 | 207 m | 20 m | — |

| c. 1955 | McGraw Tower | 141 | 25 m | 10 m | — |

On the 1914 postcard the detector keeps 16 standing people; at the most likely
pose their implied heights have median **1.76 m** (prior 1.70 ± 0.08 m),
a check the fit did not use.

**Finding (identified by inspection, not by the pipeline):** every registered
Boardman pose is a mirror image. All 22 modern photographers in the fragment
stand west or south-west of the tower, so the modern model contains only the
tower's and library's west and south faces. The postcards were taken from the
Arts Quad side, north-east of the Boardman site. McGraw Tower's faces look
alike, so the matcher paired east-face features with west-face points; the
poses fit every anchor to a few pixels and look toward the Boardman site, but
from the wrong side (Boardman appears to the right of the tower instead of the
left). An automatic line-of-sight test against lidar building heights did not
catch it, and neither did a test of whether the vanished site would have
hidden the matched anchors; both were checked and the second was removed.

**Consequence:** no bound on Boardman Hall is shown. The carving step is not
done, because carving from mirror poses would place free space on the wrong
side of the site.

## What would change these results

1. Modern photographs of McGraw Tower and Uris Library from the Arts Quad
   side. The 228-photo snapshot frozen on 2026-10-24 (38 Uris and 34 tower
   photographs) was being reconstructed when this was written.
2. An automatic handedness check for mirror poses, for example by requiring
   that the matched anchors' surface normals face the camera.
